??? info "Know the Term: Small vs large models"
    Model size is usually measured in **parameters** (the adjustable weights learned in
    training). **Large** models have hundreds of billions of parameters or more and run in big
    data centres; they are generally better at difficult reasoning, rare topics and nuanced
    writing, but are slower and cost more per token. **Small** models (often called **small
    language models**, roughly a few billion parameters or fewer) are faster and cheaper, and
    some can run on a laptop or phone without an internet connection. Providers often offer a
    family in several sizes (for example a "mini" or "flash" tier alongside a flagship, as of
    2026). For narrow, well-defined tasks, such as sorting messages into categories or pulling
    dates out of text, a small model is often good enough.

    **Analogy:** a team of fast junior assistants vs one senior expert. The juniors are cheaper
    and quicker for routine work; you call the expert for the hard cases.

    **Why you care:** bigger is not always better. Matching the model to the task saves money,
    energy and waiting time, and a small model running locally can keep sensitive data from
    leaving your organization.
