??? info "Know the Term: Multi-agent system"
    A **multi-agent system** splits a job across several agents, each with its own instructions,
    tools and a narrow slice of the information — for example a Researcher, an Analyst, a Writer
    and a Reviewer. A coordinating component (an **orchestrator** or **supervisor**) decides who
    works on what and in which order, and passes results between them. Specialists with short,
    focused instructions often outperform one agent juggling everything, but every hand-off
    costs time and money, and errors can compound from one agent to the next.

    **Analogy:** an emergency coordination cell — sector leads each own their area, and a
    coordinator assigns tasks and merges their inputs into one report.

    **Why you care:** more agents is not automatically better. Use several only when the job
    genuinely splits into parts that need different instructions, tools or information.
