??? info "Know the Term: Reasoning models"
    A **reasoning model** is a language model trained to "think before it answers". Before
    writing its final reply, it generates a long internal working-out (sometimes called
    **thinking** or a **reasoning trace**): breaking the problem into steps, trying approaches
    and checking its own work. It learned this habit through extra training that rewards correct
    final answers on problems that can be checked, such as maths, logic and code. Many tools now
    offer a "thinking" or "reasoning" mode, or switch it on automatically for harder questions
    (as of 2026). Reasoning takes more time and more tokens, so it costs more.

    **Analogy:** the difference between answering a colleague off the top of your head and
    saying "give me ten minutes with a notepad".

    **Why you care:** use reasoning modes for multi-step analysis, such as reconciling figures
    from several reports; don't bother for a quick rewrite. Reasoning reduces some errors but
    doesn't remove them, and a long, tidy chain of thought can still end in a wrong answer.
