??? info "Know the Term: Open vs closed models"
    A **closed** (or **proprietary**) model is used only through its maker's service or partners:
    you send your text over the internet and get an answer back, but you never get the model
    itself. Examples include the GPT, Claude and Gemini families (as of 2026). An
    **open-weight** model has its **weights** (the trained numbers that make up the model)
    published for download, so an organization can run it on its own servers or even a laptop,
    and adapt it. Examples include the Llama, Mistral, Qwen and DeepSeek families (as of 2026).
    "Open weights" is not always "open source": the training data and code are often not
    released, and licences can restrict use. Closed models are often the most capable and the
    easiest to use; open models give more control over where data goes and what it costs.

    **Analogy:** a closed model is like a restaurant: you get the meal, never the kitchen. An
    open-weight model is like a ready-made dish you can take home, reheat in your own kitchen and
    season to taste, though you still don't get the full recipe.

    **Why you care:** the choice affects where your data is processed, who can see it, cost and
    control. For sensitive organizational data, being able to run a model inside your own infrastructure can
    matter as much as raw capability.
